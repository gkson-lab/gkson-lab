# rules for claude in this repo

## design laws (apply to every ui change)

from "20 design laws to give claude" (charles liu). laws 18 to 20 weren't readable; add them here when known.

1. **aesthetic-usability effect**: polished, cohesive visuals make things feel easier to use. finish the details.
2. **law of proximity**: things that belong together sit together; space separates groups.
3. **von restorff effect**: the odd one stands out. give the one thing that matters (primary action, recommended option) a distinct look, and only that one.
4. **zeigarnik effect**: people remember unfinished tasks. show progress and what's left.
5. **tesler's law**: complexity can't vanish, only move. absorb it in the system, not the user.
6. **law of prägnanz**: people read the simplest form. prefer simple, clear shapes and layouts.
7. **serial position effect**: first and last items are remembered best. put key items at the start and end of lists and navs.
8. **progressive disclosure**: show the essentials first, reveal advanced stuff on demand.
9. **law of common region**: things inside a shared boundary read as a group. use regions to group, not to decorate.
10. **goal-gradient effect**: motivation rises near the finish. show how close people are.
11. **law of similarity**: things that look alike seem to work alike. same-role controls get the same style; don't style equal actions differently.
12. **recognition over recall**: show options instead of making people remember them.
13. **pareto principle**: most use comes from a few features. make those excellent and prominent.
14. **occam's razor**: the simplest solution that works wins. cut what doesn't earn its place.
15. **parkinson's law**: tasks fill the time given. keep flows short: defaults, autofill, fewer steps.
16. **uniform connectedness**: visually connected elements (lines, shared color, containers) read as related.
17. **paradox of the active user**: people skip instructions and dive in. design for learning by doing; no walls of onboarding text.
